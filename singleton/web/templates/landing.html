FAQ

Questions people ask first.

Is there a token or a coin?

No. Singleton uses a public blockchain only as a notary: set roots and revocation lists are anchored so anyone can audit them. There is no token, no airdrop, no speculation — holders are free forever and platforms pay in dollars.

Do you store my ID?

No. We verify the issuer's signature on your credential, compute keyed hashes of the document number and of name + date of birth (so nobody can enrol twice), and discard everything else. For adults we don't even keep a birthday — only the fact that age thresholds have passed.

Can apps track me across sites?

No. Your pseudonym is derived from your key and the app's scope. Two apps comparing notes see two unrelated values. Even Singleton cannot tell which holder produced a proof — the signature only shows it came from one member of an anonymity set.

What if I lose my phone?

Split your recovery seed between trusted guardians (any 3 of 5 can restore it). Recovery brings back the same key — so every app sees the same pseudonym, and you keep your accounts. You can also revoke a lost key instantly.

I don't have a passport or driver's licence.

Visit a partner location for a two-minute in-person check, or have three document-verified people vouch for you. Vouched passes prove "human" (not age), and each voucher's pseudonym is rate-limited so vouching can't be farmed.

How is this different from World ID?

No orb and no biometrics. Singleton accepts the government credentials and wallets people already have, works on any phone, and never scans a body. Like World, it gives apps an anonymous, per-app unique-human proof.

Can a teenager borrow a parent's ID?

One document can back exactly one Pass. If a parent's licence is used for a child's Pass, the parent cannot get one without revoking the child's — a strong deterrent. Platforms can additionally require document-verified passes for high-risk features.

Is this compatible with GDPR?

It is designed around data minimisation: platforms receive no personal data, only a pseudonym and predicates. Singleton processes credential data transiently at enrolment and retains keyed hashes for de-duplication only.

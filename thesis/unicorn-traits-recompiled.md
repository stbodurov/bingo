# What makes a unicorn, and the ideas re-scored against it

Oct 6, 2026. This replaces the pass/fail tournament with weights taken from published unicorn and startup-outcome research. Scores are my judgment applied to the cited evidence. They are not model outputs.

## 1. What the evidence says

| Trait | Evidence | What it means for idea selection |
|---|---|---|
| **Timing** | Idealab's study of hundreds of companies: timing explained **42%** of the difference between success and failure, ahead of team (32%), idea (28%), business model (24%) and funding (14%) ([Bill Gross, TED](https://tech.co/news/bill-gross-ted-2015-06)) | Weight a dated, external catalyst the most |
| **Real market pull** | **43%** of 431 failed VC-backed startups died of poor product-market fit; running out of cash is the symptom ([CB Insights via Segmentos](https://segmentos.io/blog/why-startups-fail)) | Demand must already exist as spend, not as a "should" |
| **Network effects** | Network effects created **~70% of tech value since 1994** ([NFX](https://www.nfx.com/post/network-effects-bible)). Firms where network effects matter are more likely to become unicorns, and platforms with global network effects carry ~26% higher valuations ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1471772724000216), [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/386239245_The_Unicorn_Phenomenon_An_Embeddedness-Based_View_of_Influencing_Factors)) | Prefer ideas where each user makes the product better for the next |
| **Growth speed** | AI-era "Shooting Stars" reach about $3M → $12M → $40M → $103M ARR in four years at ~60% gross margin; "Supernovas" reach $100M in ~1.5 years ([Bessemer State of AI](https://startupmentors.in/2026/03/17/supernovas-or-shooting-stars-what-bessemers-2025-ai-data-says-about-the-right-growth-path/)) | The idea must plausibly reach $100M of revenue in about four years |
| **Team** | 2+ founders beat solo founders by 163%; a technical cofounder lifts enterprise outcomes 230% ([First Round 10-Year Project](https://www.geekwire.com/2015/first-round-releases-10-years-of-investment-data-and-some-of-it-will-surprise-you/)). Founders aged ~30–34 and alumni of top tech companies are 2–3× more likely to build unicorns ([Strebulaev, Stanford](https://poetsandquants.com/2023/08/31/this-schools-alumni-have-created-more-than-200-billion-dollar-companies-in-the-last-28-years/)). Prior success raises the next venture's odds from 18% to 30% ([Gompers et al.](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w12592/w12592.pdf)) | Founder-market fit and a cofounder matter as much as the idea |
| **Investor fit** | a16z crypto's new $2.2B fund targets exactly five areas: **stablecoins, RWA tokenization, perpetual futures, prediction markets, on-chain finance** ("the parts of crypto actually being used") ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/05/as-crypto-cools-a16zcrypto-raises-a-2-2b-fund/)). YC's 2026 requests include "Stablecoin Financial Services" and "AI-native service companies" ([YC RFS](https://modelence.com/yc-rfs-spring-2026)) | For a web3 + AI pitch, sit inside a category top VCs are actively funding |
| **Base rate** | Only ~1% of VC-funded startups become unicorns ([CB Insights](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/381090845_Market-first_or_technology-first_Exploring_unicorns'_pathways_to_extreme_valuations)) | Expect any single idea to be a long shot; choose the one with the most traits present |

**What my earlier picks missed:** they were B2B compliance wedges with weak network effects. None sat in the five areas a16z crypto is actually funding. And they were chosen by elimination, not by how many unicorn traits they had.

## 2. Scoring method

Each idea is scored 1–5 on six traits, with weights taken from the evidence above:

| Trait | Weight |
|---|---|
| Timing / catalyst | 25% |
| Market pull (spend exists today) | 20% |
| Network effects | 20% |
| Growth speed to $100M | 15% |
| Whitespace (no dominant player in the wedge) | 10% |
| Founder-market fit (supply-chain compliance engineer) | 10% |

Web3 + AI is a filter (both must be used), not a score.

## 4. The top two, with their fatal risks

### #1 Real-economy perps: freight and tariff markets (new)

**What:** perpetual-futures and prediction markets on the prices that hit importers. These include container freight lanes (Asia–US West Coast), effective tariff rates and key input commodities. AI builds the price indices and oracles from carrier quotes, ship-tracking and customs data, and an AI hedging desk turns an importer's shipments into a hedge.

**Why it scores highest:**
- **Timing:** on-chain real-world-asset perps went from **$29B (Q1 2025) to $524B (Q1 2026)**, about $1T so far in 2026 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/content/specials/nearly-1-trillion-in-rwa-volume-on-crypto-exchanges-in-2026-how-gold-oil-and-equities-are-reshaping-what-exchanges-trade-126072900576_1.html)). Hyperliquid's permissionless-market feature (HIP-3) reached **$3.9B open interest**, and commodities are 67% of its newest-market volume ([Nansen](https://nansen.ai/post/what-is-hip-3-hyperliquid), [FinanceFeeds](https://financefeeds.com/hyperliquid-hip-3-explained/)).
- **Network effects:** liquidity begets liquidity, with speculators on one side and hedgers on the other.
- **Investor fit:** perps and prediction markets are both on a16z crypto's list. Variational raised $50M (Dragonfly) and Ostium $24M (General Catalyst, Jump) for real-world perps ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/05/21/peer-to-peer-trading-startup-variational-raises-usd50-million-for-real-world-perps-in-funding-round-led-by-dragonfly), [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/381241/harvard-alumni-founded-ostium-lands-24-million-in-fresh-funding-to-scale-onchain-perpetuals-for-rwas)).
- **Whitespace:** container freight is "one of the last major global commodities without a liquid futures market" ([Xeneta](https://www.xeneta.com/blog/container-shipping-is-one-of-the-last-major-global-commodities-without-a-liquid-futures-market.-thats-about-to-change)). ICE, Euronext and CME are now splitting what little liquidity exists ([Container Magazine](https://container-mag.com/2026/03/27/container-freight-futures-exchanges-liquidity/)). I found no on-chain freight or tariff perps. Polymarket already lists **116 tariff markets** ([CoinLaw](https://coinlaw.io/prediction-market-statistics/)).

**Fatal risks:**
1. **Prices that don't trade continuously can break perps.** Ventuals wound down after $650M of pre-IPO perp volume and a 45% SpaceX price collapse ([Bankless Times](https://www.banklesstimes.com/articles/2026/06/15/ventuals-winds-down-after-650m-in-pre-ipo-perps-volume/), [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/402991/ventuals-to-compensate-traders-after-pre-ipo-spacex-perps-plunge-45-on-hyperliquid)). Freight indices update daily or weekly, so this is a design problem to solve first.
2. **Cost of entry:** launching a market on Hyperliquid needs ~**$32M of staked HYPE**, and TradeXYZ holds 90–98% of that volume ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/hyperliquid-goes-permissionless-hip-3-143449177.html), [KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/trade-xyz-builds-92-markets-and-98-hip-3-volume-on-hyperliquid-in-8-months)). The realistic path is to be the **index and oracle provider** that existing venues list.
3. **US access:** perps for US persons are only now opening (Kalshi BTC perps; Coinbase's FCM approval) ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2026/05/28/u-s-cftc-opens-crypto-perp-door-with-approval-of-first-regulated-firm)). Real hedgers may need a regulated venue.
4. **Index licensing:** Freightos, Xeneta and NYSHEX license their indices to exchanges, so you'd need your own data-driven index.

**Cheapest test:** publish an AI-built daily freight index for 4 lanes, back-test it against the incumbent indices, and pitch it to TradeXYZ, Ostium and Variational as a listable oracle. Measure whether a venue lists it and whether traders open positions.

### #2 Recourse+: verified tariff pass-through ledger

**What:**
- Suppliers publish proofs that each surcharge equals the duty actually paid.
- Refund entitlements are committed airdrop-style, so every customer can verify and claim theirs.
- Buyers audit surcharges with the existing Recourse engine.

**Why it scores high:**
- **Timing:** about $165B of refunds are flowing, there are **80+ pass-through class actions** ([CLM](https://www.clm.com/tariff-refunds-and-pass-through-litigation-where-we-are-now-and-what-comes-next/)), and tariff regimes keep changing.
- **Network effects:** a two-sided supplier–buyer graph.
- **Whitespace:** no verified pass-through product found.
- **Founder fit:** maximal. The working MVP already exists.

**Fatal risks:**
1. **The blockchain is optional:** a transparency log could replace the chain.
2. **Revenue is lumpy:** it spikes with refund waves.
3. **Investor fit:** it's outside a16z crypto's five areas, so the pitch is "AI-native services" (Sequoia) more than crypto.

**Cheapest test:** five calls with suppliers who were sued or are exposed.

### The combined path

The two ideas share one dataset. Recourse's dated tariff rulebook and supplier graph are exactly what you need to build credible tariff and freight indices. A plausible sequence:

1. **Recourse+ for revenue now:** contingency and subscription income.
2. **Use its data to publish the indices.**
3. **List those indices as perps and prediction markets.** That's where the network effects and the a16z-style upside are.

This turns a services business into an exchange-adjacent platform, which is the path most crypto unicorns took: data and infrastructure first (Chainlink, Chainalysis), then markets.

## 5. Caveats

- The scores are judgment calls on cited evidence. A different founder would score founder fit differently.
- Several market figures come from search summaries of secondary sources. Primary pages were blocked in this environment.
- The research is unambiguous on one point: **team composition** (a cofounder, and a technical lead for enterprise) moves outcomes as much as the idea does. Any of these ideas needs a cofounder with markets or trading experience (#1) or with supplier sales experience (#2).

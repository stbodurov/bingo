# Idea tournament: web3 × AI

Oct 6, 2026. Every idea had to pass six gates. Each gate was checked by search, not assumed. An idea dies at its first clear failure.

| # | Gate | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| G1 | **Competitor** | No funded leader already owns this exact wedge |
| G2 | **Budget** | A named company or spend line proves people pay for this today |
| G3 | **Many payers** | 10k+ paying customers or 1M+ paying users is plausible |
| G4 | **Web3 + AI load-bearing** | Remove either one and the product breaks |
| G5 | **Buildable by you** | Capital-light, no heavy licensing, fits a supply-chain compliance engineer |
| G6 | **Catalyst** | A dated reason it works within 24 months |

## The honest result

**No idea passes all six gates cleanly.** The more crypto-native an idea is, the more it fails G2 (no budget yet) or G1 (Coinbase, World or a16z portfolio companies already there). The more an idea has real budgets, the more the blockchain becomes optional (G4).

That isn't a research failure. It's the actual shape of web3 × AI in late 2026. Two ideas come closest:

### Finalist A: Fontis (strict web3 + AI)

- **Why it survives:**
  - **Budget:** Equifax made $2.18B verifying income and employment in 2025.
  - **Many payers:** every renter, borrower and benefits applicant is a check.
  - **Web3 is load-bearing:** zkTLS is what makes the proof trustworthy.
  - **Catalysts:** AI-forged documents up ~5×; Medicaid work requirements on Dec 31, 2026.
- **Weak point (G1):** Argyle already reaches many gig platforms through credential-based connections, and Plaid infers income from bank data.
  - The wedge only holds if Fontis is clearly better on coverage (any site, any country) and on fraud resistance.
  - That needs proving with 5 property-manager pilots, not more desk research.

### Finalist B: Recourse+ (strongest founder fit)

**What it is:**
1. Suppliers publish a cryptographic proof that each tariff surcharge equals the duty they actually paid.
2. When refunds arrive, the refund owed to each customer is committed as a Merkle root, airdrop-style. Every buyer or consumer can verify their own entitlement and claim it, and the allocation can't be rewritten after the fact.
3. AI rebuilds the duty actually owed per SKU from the Recourse rulebook.

**Why it survives:**
- **Competitor:** no product found that offers verified pass-through.
- **Budget:** at least [80 tariff pass-through class actions](https://www.clm.com/tariff-refunds-and-pass-through-litigation-where-we-are-now-and-what-comes-next/), including suits against FedEx, UPS and Ubiquiti, mean defence spend, and $165B of refunds are flowing.
- **Many payers:** thousands of suppliers itemized surcharges.
- **Founder fit:** maximal. You already built the engine.
- **Catalyst:** refunds are flowing now, new tariff regimes keep coming, and some sellers already refund voluntarily: Panic auto-refunded $12.36 per customer.

**Weak point (G4):** a public commitment proves the allocation can't be rewritten, but a transparency log, like the ones behind HTTPS certificates, could do much of that without a blockchain. Web3 becomes essential only if refunds are paid out as stablecoin claims, or if proofs need to be checked by parties who don't trust each other.

## What to do next

Pick one finalist and validate it with buyers before building anything else:

- **Fontis:** 5 property-manager calls. Ask what share of applicants they can't verify today, what they pay per check, and whether they'd run a 500-application pilot.
- **Recourse+:** 5 calls with suppliers who were sued or are exposed. Ask what defending a pass-through suit costs, whether they'd publish surcharge proofs, and how they're handling refunds to customers.

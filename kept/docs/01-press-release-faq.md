## Press release

**Kept launches the GLP-1 companion that protects the result, not just the prescription**

*Dose reminders, side-effect coaching, protein and strength targets, and a planned off-ramp. For anyone on Zepbound, Wegovy, the Wegovy pill, Foundayo or any other GLP-1 — from any prescriber, on any coverage.*

**NEW YORK —** Roughly one in nine American adults now takes a GLP-1 medicine for weight. The drugs work. What happens around them decides whether the result lasts:

- About two-thirds of people without diabetes stop within a year, most often because of side effects or cost.
- About 60% of lost weight returns within a year of stopping.
- Without protein and resistance training, a quarter to more than a third of the weight lost can be muscle.

Kept is the daily companion that closes those gaps.

Each morning Kept shows one screen:

- When your next dose is due, with the label's missed-dose rule if you're late.
- Today's protein, fiber and water targets, computed from your adjusted body weight.
- A muscle-protection score showing how much of your loss is likely lean mass.
- A side-effect coach that learns which day after your dose symptoms peak.
- A two-tap daily check-in for appetite, food noise, energy and mood.

When the medicine gets expensive, Kept finds the cheaper legitimate route: savings cards, the $50 Medicare program, manufacturer cash prices, a lower-cost FDA-approved alternative, or a coverage appeal letter drafted from your own logs.

When it's time to step down, Kept plans the off-ramp: maintain, taper or stop, with a regain guardrail that flags a 2% trend rise early.

"My prescriber sees me for fifteen minutes every three months," says the member we built Kept for *(illustrative quote — Kept has no beta members yet)*. "Kept is the other 129,585 minutes."

**Kept is free.** Kept Plus ($9.99/month or $69/year) adds the strength program, muscle score, off-ramp planner, cost finder, appeal letters and the doctor-ready report.

**Employers and health plans** can offer Kept Plus to every covered member for $0.85 per covered life per month. Their benefits team gets a de-identified dashboard showing:

- persistence at 3, 6 and 12 months against the ~35% real-world benchmark;
- average loss;
- protein and strength adherence;
- the drug spend that is still producing results.

Groups under ten are always suppressed. Individual data never leaves the member.

Kept never prescribes, never sells medication or supplements, never sells data and never shows ads.

## Customer FAQ

**Does Kept prescribe or sell medication?**
No. Kept works with whatever your prescriber, telehealth service or pharmacy provides. We don't prescribe, compound, resell or take a cut of any medicine. That is what lets us point you to the cheapest legitimate route.

**Which medicines does it support?**
- Zepbound, Mounjaro (tirzepatide)
- Wegovy injection, Ozempic, the Wegovy pill (semaglutide)
- Foundayo (orforglipron)
- Saxenda (liraglutide)

Dose ladders, step intervals and missed-dose windows come from each label.

**Will Kept tell me to change my dose?**
Never. It tells you what the label says ("you've been on 10 mg for 9 weeks; the label's next step is 12.5 mg") and when to talk to your prescriber. If side effects were severe that week, it says many prescribers hold the dose.

**I'm already on Noom / my telehealth's app. Why Kept?**
Those apps are attached to one prescriber's business. Kept is prescriber-agnostic. It works the same whether you got your prescription from your PCP, a telehealth service or an endocrinologist, and whether you pay $25 or $449.

It's built around the three things that decide whether the result lasts: staying on treatment, keeping muscle, and planning the exit.

**What's free?**
- Dose log and reminders
- Side-effect coach with red-flag triage
- Weight trend
- Protein and water targets
- Daily check-in
- Data export

Plus adds:
- The three-day strength program
- Muscle-protection score
- Off-ramp planner
- Cost finder and appeal letters
- Doctor-ready report

**How do reminders work without an app install?**
Settings → *Add to my calendar* subscribes your phone's calendar to a private feed. You get a recurring event on dose day with an alarm, plus a "talk to your prescriber" event when the label's step-up window arrives. Native iOS/Android apps with push notifications open from the waitlist in waves.

**Is my health data safe? Who sees it?**
You do. Your employer sees only de-identified totals for groups of ten or more — never your name, never your logs.

Export everything as JSON any time. Delete your account and every log with your password.

We don't sell or share data for advertising, and there are no ads.

**What if I want to stop?**
Open *Plan & cost → Your off-ramp*. Pick maintain, taper or stop, take the plan to your prescriber, and Kept watches your trend afterwards.

## Employer & health-plan FAQ

**Why would we pay for this when we're debating whether to cover the drugs at all?**
That is exactly when. Employers have been pulling back:
- Business Group on Health's 2026 survey shows coverage for weight management falling from 72% to 60%.
- 14% have dropped or plan to drop coverage by 2027.
- Starbucks and Cigna are among the named employers stopping coverage.

The most common alternative to dropping coverage is **tightening it**: higher BMI thresholds, biometric checks and *mandatory participation in a weight-management program*.

Kept is that program — software-first, so priced at a fraction of coaching-led alternatives. It is the program that keeps the drug spend productive:
- A member who stops at month four and regains has produced almost nothing for the ~$4,000 spent.
- A member who stays on therapy, keeps muscle and tapers with a plan has produced the outcome you paid for.

**What does the dashboard show?**
- Members on a GLP-1, and active in the last 14 days.
- Persistence at 3, 6 and 12 months against the benchmark.
- Average trend loss.
- Protein-target adherence and strength sessions per week.
- Members with severe side effects (routed to care, never named).
- Medicine mix.
- Drug spend, and "drug spend kept productive".

Every figure is suppressed below ten members.

**How is it priced?**
$0.85 per covered life per month, billed on covered lives rather than users. That gives a predictable line item with no per-engagement surprises.

For 4,200 covered lives, that is $3,570 a month — about 4% of the monthly drug spend for ~85 members on therapy at $1,050.

**How do members join?**
Your benefits team shares an enrollment code. Members enter it at sign-up or in Settings and get Kept Plus at no cost. Leaving the plan returns them to Free with their logs intact.

**Do you integrate with Lilly Employer Connect, NovoCare or our PBM?**
The dashboard is source-agnostic today. Claims and PBM feeds are on the roadmap for persistence measured from fills rather than self-report (see internal FAQ).

## Internal FAQ

**Why now?**
Four things happened at once:
- **Use exploded.** Adult use roughly tripled in two years.
- **Pills arrived.** The Wegovy pill and Foundayo made GLP-1s a primary-care, cash-pay, mass-market category.
- **Cash prices fell.** Manufacturer direct and TrumpRx prices fell to $149–$449, so millions now pay out of pocket with no program around them.
- **Employers turned.** Coverage moved from "cover everything" to "cover with conditions", and the condition they reach for is program participation.

**Who are we competing with?**
- **Prescriber-attached companions** (Noom's free GLP-1 Companion launched June 2026; WeightWatchers; Ro; Found). They are funnels for their own prescribing. They can't be the neutral layer for members who got their medicine elsewhere, and they won't route members to a cheaper competitor.
- **Employer cardiometabolic programs** (Omada, Virta, Teladoc/Livongo). They are coaching-led and priced accordingly, built before GLP-1s. They bill on engagement and are moving into prescribing themselves (Omada GLP-1 Flex Care). Kept is software-first, with clinicians as the user's own.
- **Generic trackers** (MyFitnessPal, Lose It, Shotsy-style dose trackers). They log, but don't coach, don't handle the label, cost or off-ramp, and have no employer business.

**What is the moat?**
- **Neutrality.** No prescribing and no drug margin is what lets us recommend the cheapest legitimate route.
- **Persistence data.** We are building the largest prescriber-agnostic longitudinal dataset of what keeps people on treatment and what happens after stopping. Always de-identified, never sold; used to improve the product and publish outcomes.
- **Employer distribution.** Once an employer makes Kept a condition of coverage, it's sticky for the life of the benefit.

**What's the business?**
See [03-revenue-and-ads-plan.md](03-revenue-and-ads-plan.md). In short:
- Freemium consumer subscription.
- Employer/health-plan PMPM.
- No ads, no data sales, no drug margin.

**Regulatory posture?**
- **FDA.** Kept is a general-wellness and adherence-support product. It never makes dosing decisions and only restates label information, with "ask your prescriber" framing. Red-flag triage routes to care and does not diagnose.
- **Data protection.** In the direct-to-consumer channel Kept is not a HIPAA covered entity. The FTC Health Breach Notification Rule and state consumer-health-data laws (Washington's My Health My Data Act and similar) still apply, so we treat all data as sensitive health data:
  - consent-based processing;
  - no advertising pixels;
  - deletion on demand.
- **Employer and plan channel.** We sign BAAs where we act for a covered entity. The dashboard is aggregate-only, with small-cell suppression.

**What are the biggest risks?**
1. **A drugmaker or PBM bundles a free companion.** Mitigation: neutrality is the product, and employers want a vendor that isn't selling the drug.
2. **Self-reported persistence is weaker than claims data.** Mitigation: a PBM/claims feed on the roadmap; persistence defined from dose logs today, conservatively.
3. **Consumer churn when members stop the drug.** Mitigation: the off-ramp and maintenance phase are where Kept is most valuable, so stopping is not churn by design.
4. **Clinical liability.** Mitigation: label-only guidance, red-flag routing, a clinical advisory board, and no dose recommendations.

How it works

Verify each asset. Register it once. Hold it outright.

Verify

Checked against sources the borrower doesn't control

Title and lien records, VINs, bank receipts, shipment records and the account debtor's real domain. Every asset, every day, with the evidence attached to each exception.

Register

One asset, one lender

Each verified asset gets a private fingerprint built from its real-world identifiers. If another lender registers the same asset, both of you are told. Neither of you sees the other's deal.

Control · optional

Priority that a second pledge can't undo

New originations can be issued as controllable electronic records under UCC Article 12. The lender holding control has priority over any filing, and the asset can't be pledged twice.

Article 12 took effect in New York on 3 June 2026 and has been enacted in 33 states and DC.

Your data

The registry knows an asset exists. Not whose it is.

Fingerprints are computed from identifiers like a VIN and obligor, or a debtor, purchase order and amount, and can't be reversed into those identifiers. They're recorded on a public ledger so no single company, us included, controls the record of who registered what first.

When two lenders register the same asset, each is told there's a collision. Who the other lender is stays private until both choose to talk.

Questions

What lenders ask first

What does the borrower have to share?

Read-only access to the loan tape or receivables aging, servicing exports and the collections account. Most facility agreements already require this for reporting and field exams.

Who can see our data?

Only your team. The registry stores fingerprints, not borrower names, amounts or deal terms. A collision tells the two lenders involved that it exists, and nothing more until they choose to talk.

Is this a crypto product?

No tokens, and nothing to trade. Verification and alerts work like any other software service. The registry records fingerprints on a public ledger so that no single company controls the record. The optional control layer uses the same ledger to give lenders control of an asset under UCC Article 12.

Does it replace field exams?

It covers every asset daily, which a field exam can't. Many lenders will keep an annual exam at first. The aim is for the exam to confirm what you already know.

How is it priced?

On the collateral you monitor, in basis points per year, with a minimum per facility. Design partners get their first facility checked at no cost.

# How to advertise Clearlien

## Who you are selling to

The buyer is a small, senior and reachable group:

- heads of **lender finance**, **warehouse lending**, **asset-based lending**, **ABF / specialty finance** and **portfolio risk**
- at **private credit funds**, **US banks with lending books to nonbanks** ($1.4T of loans, 86% held by banks with $100B+ in assets), and **factors**

That is a few thousand people in the US, not millions. One customer is worth $250k–$1M a year.

**Goal for the first 90 days:** 3–5 design partners from about 40 qualified sign-ups. These are targets, not forecasts. A qualified sign-up is a lender, not a vendor or student, monitoring $500M or more of collateral.

The consequences:

- Spend on precision, not reach. A $300 cost per qualified lead is cheap at this deal size.
- With lenders, lead with the fraud cases and the free facility check, then explain the ledger as the reason a second pledge can't hide. With on-chain platforms, lead with the Oracle and lien tokens.
- Founder-led outreach will beat every paid channel at first. Paid channels support it.

## Channels, in order of expected return

### 1. Founder-led outbound ($0–$150/month)

- **Build a list of about 300 target firms.** Sources:
  - banks reporting lending to nonbanks (call-report NDFI disclosures)
  - private credit managers with ABF or lender-finance strategies
  - members of the Secured Finance Network, the Structured Finance Association and the International Factoring Association
  - lenders named as creditors in the First Brands and Tricolor bankruptcy filings, which are public court records
- **Find two or three people per firm** with LinkedIn Sales Navigator (about $100/month). Use Apollo or Clay for work emails.
- **Send a three-touch sequence over 10 days**, short and specific. Example first touch:

  > Subject: 3,225 loans in two securitizations
  >
  > The SEC alleges Tricolor put the same 3,225 auto loans into two deals at once. Field exams sampled the borrower's own files and didn't catch it.
  >
  > We're building Clearlien to check every asset in a borrowing base against title records and bank receipts daily, and flag anything registered to another lender. Design partners get one facility checked free.
  >
  > Worth 20 minutes? Early access: [link?utm_source=email&utm_medium=outbound&utm_campaign=tricolor]

- **Follow the law.** US cold email must include your physical address and an opt-out (CAN-SPAM). For UK and EU contacts, email only named business addresses with a legitimate-interest basis and honor opt-outs immediately.

### 2. LinkedIn: founder posts and targeted ads ($2,500–$5,000/month)

- **Founder posts** two or three times a week. Short breakdowns of each public case: what the collateral was, which control missed it, which independent check would have caught it. People in this audience read and forward this kind of post.
- **Ads:**
  - **Targeting:** job titles containing lender finance, warehouse lending, asset-based lending, specialty finance, structured finance, asset-backed, credit risk. Seniority Director and up. Industries: banking, investment management, capital markets, financial services. Also upload your 300-firm list as a company-list audience.
  - **Formats:** Document Ads (a 6-page "Double-pledging playbook" PDF) and Lead Gen Forms (pre-filled, which converts better than sending people to the page). Message Ads to the company list.
  - **Expect** roughly $8–20 per click and $150–$400 per lead for senior finance audiences. Judge leads by qualification rate, not volume.
- **Ad copy to test:**
  - "Your field exam samples the borrower's files once a year. Clearlien checks every asset against title records and bank receipts every morning. Free check of one facility for design partners."
  - "Is any asset in your borrowing base pledged to another lender? Today you can't know. Join early access."
  - "The SEC alleges 3,225 auto loans were pledged into two securitizations at once. Would your monitoring catch that?"

### 3. A lead magnet that does the selling ($0, a week of work)

- **"The double-pledging playbook":** a short report on how the First Brands, Tricolor, HPS and MFS collateral failed, with 12 checks that would have caught each one. Gate it with the waitlist form.
- **Free "duplicate collateral checker":** a browser tool that compares two loan tapes and lists repeated VINs or loan IDs. It runs entirely in the browser, so no data leaves the user's machine. It is a strong opener for outbound and LinkedIn, and every user is a qualified lead.

### 4. Earned media ($0)

- Pitch the reporters already covering these cases (Bloomberg's credit team, FT Alphaville, ABF Journal, Asset Securitization Report, Creditflux) with something new.
- For example: an analysis of public ABS filings for repeated collateral, or a guest piece on UCC Article 12 ("control beats filing") and what it means for lenders who rely only on filings.

### 5. Newsletters and podcasts (about $1,000–$5,000 per placement; ask for rate cards)

- ABF Journal, Asset Securitization Report, Creditflux, 9fin, PitchBook's private-credit coverage, Fintech Business Weekly, Net Interest.
- Guest spots on private-credit podcasts cost nothing and reach the same people.

### 6. Events (travel only at first; no booths yet)

- SFNet Annual Convention, SFA's SFVegas, ABS East and the IFA Annual Factoring Conference. Check this year's dates.
- Book meetings in advance from the outbound list. Host one small dinner for 8–10 lender-finance heads. Skip booths until you have customers to show.

### 7. Google Search ($500–$1,000/month, small but high intent)

- Keywords: collateral verification, double pledging, borrowing base audit, field exam services, loan tape audit, warehouse line due diligence.
- Low volume, but people searching these terms are in the market.

### 8. Partners (revenue share)

- **Field-exam firms and backup servicers** who want a software layer, and **secured-transactions law firms** advising on Article 12.
- Join the **SFA fraud-mitigation working groups**. This is how the registry becomes a standard.

### 9. The on-chain credit segment (Oracle buyers)

Tokenization platforms and on-chain lending protocols are the second audience, and here crypto channels *are* the right ones:

- **Direct outreach to roughly 40 teams.** Private-credit protocols, RWA tokenization platforms, and DeFi lending markets that list tokenized-credit collateral.
- **Public analysis on X and crypto research forums.** For example, "how much tokenized private credit could be double-pledged off-chain?" Tag protocol founders. Publish in RWA newsletters and on research forums.
- **Governance proposals.** Lending protocols decide which collateral to accept through governance votes. A proposal to require oracle attestations for RWA collateral puts Clearlien in front of the exact decision makers.
- **Events:** RWA and tokenization tracks at crypto conferences (Devconnect, Permissionless, RWA summits). Check this year's dates.
- **Use the same UTM scheme,** for example `?utm_source=x&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=oracle`.

### What not to spend on

- Crypto media, X ads and crypto conferences *for the lender audience*. Bank and private-credit buyers aren't there. Use them only for the on-chain segment above.
- Reddit, Product Hunt and broad Meta or Google Display. Wrong audience for both segments.
- Booths before you have a customer story.

**Review weekly:**

1. Sign-ups by source.
2. Qualified rate.
3. Calls booked.
4. Design partners signed.

Cut any channel whose cost per *qualified* lead is above about $600 after $2,000 of spend.

## First 30 days

| Week | Do |
|---|---|
| 1 | Domain, deploy the page, privacy policy, analytics. Build the 300-firm list. Write the playbook PDF. Founder LinkedIn profile rewritten around the problem |
| 2 | Start outbound (30 contacts a day). First two case-breakdown posts. LinkedIn campaign live at $100/day with two ad variants |
| 3 | Publish the playbook; retarget site visitors with it. Pitch two reporters. Book event meetings |
| 4 | Kill the weaker ad. Double down on the best outbound subject line. Book the first facility checks from sign-ups that asked for one |

**Budget for the first 90 days:** about $3,000–$6,000 a month, most of it on LinkedIn. Outbound, the lead magnet, PR and partners cost time, not money.
